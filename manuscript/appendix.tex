\section*{Exclusion of Large Language Model Approaches}
\label{app:llm_exclusion}

Large language models have become an active direction in SVG generation. 
Methods such as StarVector, OmniSVG, LLM4SVG, and InternSVG treat SVG 
as structured code and generate it through transformer architectures 
pretrained on large-scale text and image collections. Commercial 
services have followed the same path: SVGMaker.io, for instance, 
generates SVG graphics from text prompts using an LLM-based pipeline 
without exposing the underlying architecture or training details.

SVG is sequential XML, so language models are a natural structural fit 
for the format. The visual quality of recent LLM-based outputs has 
improved substantially over earlier sequence models. However, these 
approaches are excluded from the main analysis for a specific reason: 
the core question of this thesis is how architectural decisions in 
representation and supervision shape the structure of generated SVGs. 
In optimization-based and dataset-driven methods, that relationship 
can be traced directly. A parametric representation produces a certain 
kind of geometry. A diffusion-based loss pushes optimization in a 
specific direction. The connection between design choice and structural 
outcome is observable.

In LLM-based generation, that connection is distributed across billions 
of parameters. The model generates SVG tokens, but why it chose a 
particular path structure, how it decides on primitive count, or what 
internal signal drives layer organization cannot be isolated in the 
same way. This does not make LLM approaches less valuable. It makes 
them less suited to the kind of representation-level analysis this 
thesis pursues. A systematic comparison of LLM-based SVG generation 
methods, including both open-source and commercial systems, remains an 
important direction for future work. Malashenko et al.~\cite{LeveragingLargeLanguage} 
provide an initial survey of this space.




\chapter{Table of chosen approaches}

The following tables summarize the fourteen models analyzed in this thesis. 
Optimization-based models are listed first, followed by implicit neural methods and 
dataset-trained methods. For optimization-based methods, "trainables" refers to per-instance 
SVG parameters refined at inference time rather than learned model weights. 
Models listed as using semantic distillation (SDS or VSD) optimize vector parameters 
at inference time using gradients backpropagated from a frozen pretrained diffusion model. 
No training dataset is required for these methods. 
Dataset-driven models learn generation behavior during training and produce SVGs through 
a forward pass at inference.
