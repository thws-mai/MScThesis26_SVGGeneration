\chapter{Comparative Synthesis}
\label{ch:synthesis}

The preceding chapters established what SVG generation methods do 
and how they should be evaluated. This chapter examines what they 
reveal when compared directly. Using the representational and 
supervisory dimensions introduced in Chapters~\ref{ch:representation} 
and~\ref{ch:supervision}, and the evaluation framework from 
Chapter~\ref{ch:Evaluation}, the analysis identifies recurring 
patterns across the fourteen models that explain why certain 
approaches produce outputs that are structurally clean and editable 
while others prioritize visual realism at the cost of usability.

The findings are organized around three observations: how 
representation and supervision interact to shape structural 
outcomes, how the realism-editability trade-off manifests 
concretely across models, and what conditions lead to 
outputs that are both visually plausible and structurally 
usable.

\section{How Representation and Supervision Shape Structure}
\label{sec:rep_sup_interaction}

The central analytical finding of this thesis is that 
representation and supervision do not act independently 
on the structure of generated SVGs. The same supervision 
signal produces different geometric outcomes depending on 
the representation it operates through, and the same 
representation produces different outputs depending on 
what signal drives it. Table~\ref{tab:representation_outcomes} 
maps these combinations across all fourteen models; the 
patterns visible in that table are the subject of the 
analysis that follows.

Consider what happens when diffusion-based supervision 
(SDS or VPSD) meets different representations. Applied to 
explicit B\'{e}zier paths, as in VectorFusion and SVGDreamer, 
it drives primitive count upward because the only way to 
approximate high-frequency visual detail with unconstrained 
parametric paths is to add more of 
them~\cite{jainVectorFusionTexttoSVGAbstracting2022, 
xingSVGDreamerTextGuided2024}. Applied to a neural implicit 
space, as in NeuralSVG, the same class of signal produces 
comparable visual quality with a fixed set of 16 
shapes~\cite{polaczekNeuralSVGImplicitRepresentation2025}, 
because the representation itself constrains how geometry 
can be arranged. The supervision did not change. The 
representation did, and with it the entire structural 
character of the output.

The same pattern appears with reconstruction losses. LIVE 
and SAMVG both minimize pixel error through 
DiffVG~\cite{maLayerwiseImageVectorization2022, 
zhuSAMVGMultistageImage2023}. LIVE initializes randomly and 
ends up with 160 overlapping paths. SAMVG initializes from 
segmentation masks and produces cleaner boundaries with 
fewer primitives (Chapter~\ref{ch:supervision}, 
Figure~\ref{fig:init_comparison}). The loss function is 
identical. What changed is where optimization started, 
which shaped what the representation converged toward.

Dataset-trained methods show the reverse pattern: 
representation is constrained by design, and structural 
quality comes from what the training data contains rather 
than from the supervision signal directly. DeepSVG and 
DeepIcon produce compact, well-organized icons because 
the SVG-Icons8 dataset consists of simple graphics with 
small path 
counts~\cite{carlierDeepSVGHierarchicalGenerative2020}. 
The reconstruction loss did not teach them to be tidy. The 
data did. LayerTracer takes this further: its training 
data encodes step-by-step construction order, so the model 
absorbs layering as a structural convention rather than 
discovering it through 
optimization~\cite{songLayerTracerCognitiveAlignedLayered2025}. 
SVGFusion and T2V-NPR occupy a middle position, combining 
learned latent structure with semantic supervision, which 
produces outputs that are more coherent than unconstrained 
optimization but bounded by the training 
distribution~\cite{xingSVGFusionScalableTexttoSVG2025, 
zhangTexttoVectorGenerationNeural2024}.

Two conclusions follow from these comparisons. First, 
representations that constrain decomposition (implicit 
fields, learned latent spaces, layered sequences) 
consistently produce cleaner outputs than representations 
that leave primitive count unconstrained (explicit 
parametric paths). This holds regardless of what 
supervision signal is applied 
(Table~\ref{tab:repr_consequences}). Second, supervision 
operating within a structured representation space produces 
more stable geometry than supervision operating directly 
in raster space. NeuralSVG using SDS within an implicit 
representation, and T2V-NPR using VSD within a learned 
path latent, both exhibit this 
effect~\cite{polaczekNeuralSVGImplicitRepresentation2025, 
zhangTexttoVectorGenerationNeural2024}. The interaction 
between these two dimensions, not either one alone, 
determines whether a generated SVG is structurally usable.

\section{The Realism--Editability Trade-off}
\label{sec:tradeoff}

The most consistent pattern across the fourteen models is the 
tension between visual realism and editability. This tension 
is not theoretical, it is observable in specific models and 
has direct practical consequences.

Methods driven by diffusion-based supervision achieve visually 
convincing outputs by refining geometry until the rendered image 
aligns with learned image priors. This refinement increases 
structural complexity in ways that undermine usability. LIVE, 
following the optimization protocol also adopted by VectorFusion, 
represents a single image using up to 160 paths distributed 
across multiple layers~\cite{maLayerwiseImageVectorization2022}. At this scale, 
selecting or modifying a single visual component requires 
identifying and adjusting dozens of overlapping primitives in 
standard design tools. SVGDreamer++ explicitly acknowledges this 
failure mode, noting that optimization-based approaches produce 
outputs where objects are entangled across paths, making 
component-level editing effectively 
impossible~\cite{xingSVGDreamerAdvancingEditability2025}.
However, this structural cost comes with a genuine visual benefit, 
as Figure~\ref{fig:realism_editability} illustrates.

This structural cost comes with a genuine visual benefit. 
VectorFusion and SVGDreamer consistently produce the most 
visually rich and semantically detailed outputs among the 
analyzed methods~\cite{jainVectorFusionTexttoSVGAbstracting2022, xingSVGDreamerTextGuided2024}.
The unconstrained primitive growth guided by strong diffusion priors that 
fragments structures is precisely what enables 
photorealistic rendering quality. The trade-off is genuine: 
neither side is simply better. The appropriate choice depends 
on whether the intended use prioritizes visual output quality 
or downstream editability. The multi-dimensional framework in 
Table~\ref{tab:framework} makes this trade-off 
quantifiable: models that score highest on semantic 
alignment (VectorFusion, SVGDreamer) consistently 
score lowest on path economy and editability.

Models that constrain representation tend to produce outputs 
that are easier to edit, even when visual abstraction is 
introduced. NeuralSVG represents complex scenes using a small 
fixed number of layers, improving usability despite reduced 
photorealistic detail~\cite{polaczekNeuralSVGImplicitRepresentation2025}. 
Dataset-trained models such as DeepSVG and DeepIcon inherit 
structural simplicity from their training data, producing 
directly editable outputs bounded by the style distribution 
of the dataset~\cite{carlierDeepSVGHierarchicalGenerative2020}.

LayerTracer represents a distinct route toward editability. 
Rather than constraining an optimization process, it is trained 
directly on layered construction sequences that reflect how 
designers build SVG graphics step by 
step~\cite{songLayerTracerCognitiveAlignedLayered2025}. 
Editability is not an emergent property but a learned one, 
the model produces layered outputs because it was explicitly 
supervised to construct graphics that way. This makes 
LayerTracer structurally closer to designer workflows than 
any optimization-based method analyzed here.



The realism--editability trade-off emerges from the interaction 
between supervision strength and representational constraints. 
When supervision strongly prioritizes visual similarity without 
structural regularization, primitive counts increase and 
editability degrades. When representation enforces decomposition 
or limits complexity, editability improves but visual detail 
is reduced. Hybrid approaches that integrate structural priors 
with semantic guidance, as in NeuralSVG and T2V-NPR, 
suggest that both objectives can be partially reconciled, though 
no current model satisfies both simultaneously at the level 
required for professional design workflows.


\section{Conditions for Clean and Editable SVG}
\label{sec:conditions}

Across the fourteen models, five conditions consistently 
correlate with cleaner and more editable SVG outputs. These 
are not isolated design choices but interacting factors that 
together determine whether a generated SVG is structurally 
usable.

\textbf{Primitive count control.} Methods that limit path 
count, either through explicit budgets, latent space 
constraints, or neural parameterization, produce outputs 
with less redundancy. Unconstrained primitive growth, common 
in pixel-supervised optimization, is the primary source of 
structural fragmentation.~\cite{maLayerwiseImageVectorization2022,
xingSVGDreamerAdvancingEditability2025}

\textbf{Semantically aligned decomposition.} SVGs become 
more editable when primitives correspond to meaningful visual 
components rather than arbitrary geometric fragments. 
Initialization strategies that use segmentation or saliency 
maps~\cite{zhuSAMVGMultistageImage2023, 
polaczekNeuralSVGImplicitRepresentation2025} and 
architectures that model layer-wise 
construction~\cite{songLayerTracerCognitiveAlignedLayered2025} 
both encourage this alignment.

\textbf{Constrained supervision domain.} When supervision 
acts within a structured representation space rather than 
directly in raster space, geometric behavior is more 
regulated. T2V-NPR and NeuralSVG both demonstrate 
this~\cite{zhangTexttoVectorGenerationNeural2024, 
polaczekNeuralSVGImplicitRepresentation2025}.

\textbf{Informed initialization.} Random initialization 
with unconstrained optimization leads to unstable structure. 
Semantically meaningful starting points reduce the need for 
excessive primitive refinement and improve structural 
coherence from the 
outset~\cite{zhuSAMVGMultistageImage2023, 
xingSVGDreamerTextGuided2024}.

\textbf{Design-aware architectural priors.} Models that 
incorporate layering, sequential construction, or scene 
composition logic produce outputs that more closely reflect 
designer intent. LayerTracer is the clearest 
example~\cite{songLayerTracerCognitiveAlignedLayered2025}.

No current model satisfies all five conditions simultaneously. 
NeuralSVG comes closest among optimization-based methods. 
LayerTracer comes closest among dataset-trained methods. 
The gap between them reflects the broader realism--editability 
trade-off: NeuralSVG produces more flexible outputs but requires 
more compute, while LayerTracer produces consistently structured 
outputs bounded by its training distribution.

A related limitation is domain coverage. Dataset-trained 
models are bounded by their training distribution: DeepSVG 
and DeepIcon generate only icons, T2V-NPR produces 
stylized non-photorealistic outputs, and SVGFusion 
operates within the scope of its SVGX training 
corpus~\cite{carlierDeepSVGHierarchicalGenerative2020, 
bingDeepIconHierarchicalNetwork2024, 
zhangTexttoVectorGenerationNeural2024, 
xingSVGFusionScalableTexttoSVG2025}. Optimization-based 
methods handle broader visual domains through their 
pretrained diffusion priors, but at the structural costs 
described above. No current model in the analyzed set 
covers specialized design domains such as typography, 
technical illustration, or character design.


\section{Implications for Future Work}
\label{sec:implications}

Two directions follow from the analysis.

The first is tighter integration between representation design 
and supervision. The analysis consistently shows that supervision 
operating within constrained representations produces better 
structural outcomes than supervision operating in raster space. 
Future models that explicitly co-design representation 
constraints and supervision signals, rather than treating 
them as independent architectural decisions, are likely to 
make more progress on the realism--editability trade-off than 
approaches that improve either dimension alone.

The second is the formalization of editability as a training 
objective rather than an evaluation afterthought. LayerTracer 
demonstrates that models can be trained directly on designer 
construction logic, producing editability by design rather than 
by constraint. Extending this approach to richer scene types, 
larger datasets, and more expressive representations would 
address the generalization limitations that currently bound 
dataset-trained methods. Combining this with the geometric 
stability advantages of neural implicit representations 
represents a direction that none of the fourteen analyzed 
models has yet explored fully.
